# Source Accounts

Scroll the Feed and you will notice accounts like **freeCodeCamp** marked with a small **Source badge** next to their name. These are **Source accounts** — automated curators that pipe high-quality developer content into Peermeld so your feed is valuable even while your own network is still small.

## What a Source account is

- **Automated, not human** — Sources aggregate content (tutorials, videos, release notes) from trusted channels.
- **Badged for transparency** — the Source badge (a small icon beside the name, as seen on `@freecodecamp`) distinguishes them from human members.
- **Followable like any profile** — following a Source routes its posts into your [FOLLOWING](./tabs-and-filters) tab.
- **Listed in the directory** — they appear in [/grow](https://peermeld.com/grow) labelled *sources*, so you can discover them alongside people.

## The official Sources

| Source            | Handle        | What it brings to your feed                  |
| ----------------- | ------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| freeCodeCamp      | `@freecodecamp` | Full-length tutorials & courses (e.g. *Python for Engineers & Robotics*) |
| Fireship          | `@fireship`   | Fast-paced tech news & code explainers        |
| Chrome Developer  | `@chromedeveloper` | Web platform updates & browser dev news   |
| Astro             | `@astro`      | Releases & guides from the Astro ecosystem    |
| Next.js           | `@nextjs`     | Framework releases & best practices           |
| Node.js           | `@node`       | Runtime releases & server-side news           |
| Peermeld System   | `@system`     | Official platform announcements               |

<Callout type="info">
  Source accounts are clearly badged and labelled "sources" or "system" — Peermeld keeps automated
  content transparent so the human community stays the star of the feed.
</Callout>

## How to use Sources well

<Steps>
<Step>

### Follow 2–4 Sources matching your stack
A Next.js developer gets the most from `@nextjs`, `@chromedeveloper` and `@fireship`; a Python learner from `@freecodecamp`. Your FOLLOWING tab becomes a personalised dev-newspaper.

</Step>
<Step>

### Engage with Source posts like any post
Upvotes and comments on Source posts are visible to everyone who follows that Source — a smart, zero-pressure way to be seen by a big audience of peers in your niche.

</Step>
<Step>

### Let Sources seed your streak
On quiet days, reacting to a Source tutorial keeps your daily [streak](../rewards/streaks) alive while genuinely teaching you something.

</Step>
</Steps>

## Why Sources exist

New networks have a cold-start problem: an empty feed. Sources make day one valuable — tutorials, release news and curated reads from minute one — while the human community grows around them. Over time your FOLLOWING tab naturally shifts toward people as your network fills in.
