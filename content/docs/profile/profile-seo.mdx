# Profile SEO

Peermeld profiles are public, fast and crawlable by design. Combined with your custom subdomain, that makes `yourname.peermeld.com` the *easiest-to-rank personal website you will ever own*. Peermeld even ships a built-in **SEO Preview** ("See exactly how your profile looks on Google and other search engines") when you claim your subdomain. Here is how to exploit it fully.

## The ranking assets you control

<Steps>
<Step>

### The URL itself
`yourname.peermeld.com` puts your *name* in the domain — the single strongest relevance signal for "your name" searches. Claim the exact name people will Google; rules are in [Claim your Subdomain](../getting-started/claim-subdomain).

</Step>
<Step>

### Title tag = name + headline
Your display name and headline render into your page title and meta description — the text Google shows in results. *"Nazmul Hossain | Software Engineer | Peermeld"* is a real Peermeld title tag. Front-load: `Name — Role — Specialty`.

</Step>
<Step>

### Bio as body copy
Search engines read your bio as page content. Naturally include the keywords you want to rank for: role, stack, industry, city. *"Full-stack developer building SaaS in Dhaka — React, Node, PostgreSQL"* covers four keyword families in one line.

</Step>
<Step>

### Fresh, indexable content
Every post, project and portfolio block adds crawlable text under your profile. Consistent activity = consistent recrawl = ranking freshness. Your [streak](../rewards/streaks) is secretly an SEO strategy.

</Step>
<Step>

### Backlinks to your subdomain
Link `yourname.peermeld.com` from your GitHub profile, X/LinkedIn bios, dev.to/Hashnode profiles, personal site and email signature. Each is a free, relevant backlink — and backlinks remain the web's core ranking currency.

</Step>
</Steps>

## Use the SEO Preview

When you claim (or revisit) your subdomain on [peermeld.com](https://peermeld.com), the **SEO Preview** panel shows your Google snippet: title, URL, description. Check:

- Does the title fit (~60 characters) without truncation?
- Does the description (~155 characters) sell a click, not just describe?
- Is your *name* the first word?

Rewrite your headline/bio until the preview makes you want to click.

## Keyword strategy for professionals

| Keyword type      | Examples                                       | Where to place it            |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Name              | "Jane Dev", "janedev"                          | Subdomain, username, name    |
| Role              | "frontend engineer", "product designer"        | Headline, bio, posts         |
| Stack             | "React", "Figma", "Kubernetes"                 | Bio, project tech tags, posts |
| Niche/industry    | "fintech", "AI agents", "design systems"       | Bio, build logs, projects    |
| Intent            | "hire", "freelance", "open to collaborate"     | Bio (only if true!)          |

<Callout type="warn" title="Don't keyword-stuff">
  Google's 2026 spam systems punish robotic keyword lists. Write for humans first — one natural
  mention per keyword family is enough. Peermeld's own profiles rank because they are genuinely
  useful pages, not because they stuff terms.
</Callout>

## Beyond Google

The same fundamentals carry to Bing, DuckDuckGo, and AI answer engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude), which heavily favour clearly-structured public pages with consistent naming — exactly what your Peermeld profile is. Keep your name, handle and bio consistent across every platform and answer engines will start connecting the dots for you.

## The 15-minute SEO audit

Run this monthly:

1. Google `your name` — does your Peermeld page appear? Top 3?
2. Google `your name + role` — present?
3. Check the SEO Preview after any profile edit.
4. Fix any dead links on your profile/portfolio/projects.
5. Publish at least one feed post this week (freshness signal).
